## Tier A — gaps that misdirect the programme

**A1. Off-page authority is never measured, yet the document uses it as its own explanation of last resort.**
§7.3 #14 concludes: "On the one prompt where URL and title are both right, placement is mid-pack. That points away from on-page targeting and toward **site-level authority** and the crawlability ceiling." Site-level authority is then never quantified anywhere in §7, §8 or §9. There is no backlink profile, no referring-domain count, no comparison against Jacquie Lawson / American Greetings / 123cards / top10, no lost-link analysis, no toxic-link check, and no assessment of how the seven-host split (www, m., blog., help., info., studio., 123g.us) fragments whatever equity a 29-year-old Intel-Capital-funded domain accumulated. §8 lists thirteen gaps and this is not one of them — which is the tell. None of it requires fetching the site, so the sandbox-egress excuse (gap 1) does not apply; Ahrefs/Majestic/Semrush/Moz/Common Crawl webgraph are all third-party. **Do:** add it as gap 0 and as a Week 1 item. If referring domains are healthy, the "authority ceiling" line in #14 is wrong and the on-page work is under-prioritised. If they are not, half the §5 content plan (fifteen pages competing against The Knot, Indeed, Hallmark) is not winnable at any content quality and should be re-scoped.

**A2. The May 14 cliff is mentioned once, in passing, and never diagnosed.**
It appears only inside gap 2 ("no way to attribute the May 14 cliff"). A traffic collapse is almost certainly why this engagement exists, and the audit contains no hypothesis list, no ranking of causes against available evidence, no check of the Google update calendar for that window, no comparison of the cliff date against any site change, host change, ad-stack change, or mobile-first re-evaluation. Every recommendation here is a general-hygiene recommendation; none is targeted at the one event the client cares about. **Do:** add a dedicated section. Enumerate candidates (core/spam/HCU update timing, manual action, ad-policy enforcement, hosting or template deploy, m.-host indexing change, Discover loss, referral-source loss), state what evidence each would leave, and tie each to a specific check in the Week 1 list. Note explicitly which of them GSC's 16-month history *will* resolve retroactively the day P0-0 lands — that materially raises P0-0's value and the document never says it.

**A3. Manual-action and search-spam policy exposure is never named as a risk.**
The document recommends deleting Product schema, fake `0.00` Offers, misused `reviewCount` and `aggregateRating` "in the same commit" for AEO-tidiness reasons. Fabricated `aggregateRating` on a template is a structured-data policy violation eligible for a rich-results manual action. Simultaneously the estate has 6,000–12,000 facet URLs, ~30,000 thin card pages, a `/tags/` space generating pages that rank for the wrong intent, and five unsecured mirror hosts serving duplicate content — a recognisable scaled-content/doorway profile. The word "penalty" does not appear. Neither does "Manual Actions" as a *deliverable* (it is listed in gap 2 only as a report the audit couldn't see). **Do:** name the policy risk explicitly, make "screenshot the Manual Actions and Security Issues reports" the first output of P0-0 with a same-day escalation path, and re-frame the schema deletion as risk remediation rather than housekeeping — it changes who signs off and how fast. Also apply this to P3 programmatic expansion: the "genuinely distinct 40–60 word answer block" gate is a quality gate, not a policy gate, and scaled content abuse should be stated as the reason.

**A4. Week 1 contains an operational break.** 
P0-2(b): "HTTP auth or IP allowlist on **all five** `123g.us` hosts", alongside P0-2(a) noindexing `media.` and `search.` vhosts. `media.` and `search.` are names of production infrastructure, and §7.3 #26 shows `h-source.123g.us` serving *identical content* to www — i.e. these hosts are wired into something. The document nowhere verifies that no production page, template, CDN rule or email references them. Auth-walling an asset host takes the site down; noindexing it does not. **Do:** split P0-2(b) into (i) a dependency audit — grep templates, CDN config, email templates, and the app for `123g.us`, `media.`, `search.` — and (ii) the lockdown, gated on (i). Order matters and the current table has them as one trivial/small row.

**A5. Mobile-first indexing versus a content plan built entirely on www.**
The document states m. is the mobile-first-indexed host (gap 6, §3, P0-7). It then specifies fifteen new §5 question pages, P1-8 "what to write" blocks, P1-9 conquest pages, the 6.2.3 seniors guide and the §6.2.5 blog work — all on www or blog., with no statement of whether any of it exists on m., how the www↔m. switch treats a URL with no mobile counterpart, or whether Google will index content that the mobile-first host does not serve. P1-2 ("absolute https alternate + reciprocal canonical") is a plumbing fix, not an answer to this. If m. serves a redirect-to-homepage or a 404 for these new paths, the entire content workstream is being built on a host that is not the indexed one. **Do:** make "does m. serve a counterpart for an arbitrary new www path, and what does the switch do on miss?" an explicit output of P0-7, and add it as a hard prerequisite on §5 page 1, not on P1-2.

**A6. The primary KPI is a metric the document says cannot measure the thing it is named for.**
§7.1: "This is **not** output from ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot or AI Overviews… What it cannot tell you: actual citation rates in any specific product." §7.5 then designates own-content-page citation rate on that same proxy as "**The primary metric**". Gap 5 defers real engine queries indefinitely, and §7.5 defers them again to "when the entity and PR work lands." Running 30 prompts against four engines is an afternoon and is the *only* surface that shows the sentiment language §6.2 is entirely built around ("dated, ads everywhere"). **Do:** move live-engine measurement into Week 1 as a baseline, not a later addition, and either demote the proxy metric to a leading indicator or rename it to what it measures (retrievable-corpus depth).

**A7. Statistical validity of the benchmark, and of its monthly deltas, is never addressed.**
n=27, run once, one locale, one session, no repeat trials, no variance estimate. 9/27 = 33%; a single prompt flipping moves it 3.7pp. §7.5 charts five derived metrics monthly and treats movement as attributable. It is not distinguishable from noise at this n. Separately, the headline 59% SoV is inflated by brand queries — being named on "is 123greetings safe" and "is 123greetings still free" is not share of voice. Strip the three brand prompts and non-brand SoV is 13/24, and the own-content-page rate on non-brand intent is lower than 33%. The decomposition table is good and does half this job; it stops short of the number that matters. **Do:** state the minimum detectable effect at n=27, add non-brand-only variants of all five metrics, run each prompt 2–3 times at baseline to size run-to-run variance, and — critically — **track competitor SoV in the same table**. Going from 33% to 38% while Jacquie Lawson goes from 70% to 85% is a loss, and the current scoreboard would record it as a win.

**A8. The single biggest ask of the business is made with zero business data.**
P2-8 asks Revenue Ops to strip interstitial and anchor ads from `viewecard` and card views — the recipient-side pageview is the highest-volume surface on an ad-monetised free product, i.e. this is plausibly the company's main revenue line. §8 gap 3 concedes the trade "cannot be evaluated" without GA4. The roadmap schedules it anyway, in days 31–60, sized "medium," with no revenue estimate, no alternative (below-fold, sender-side-only, frequency-capped, first-view-exempt), no A/B design, and no fallback if the answer is "this costs 40% of revenue." §6.2 then makes the ad fix load-bearing for Tier 3 PR ("needs the ad-fix news hook"). **Do:** convert P2-8 into a measured experiment with an explicit revenue guardrail and a named decision-maker, and mark every downstream item that assumes it ships. More broadly: the document has no revenue or traffic sizing anywhere, so nothing is prioritised by business impact — §8 gap 3 says so, honestly, but the roadmap then presents itself as a priority order regardless.

## Tier B — real defects in the plan as written

**B1. The 90-day exit criterion is unachievable by the document's own dependency arithmetic.**
"90-day exit criteria… **did own-content-page citation rate move off 33%?**" P0-6 rolls to 100% at day 61+ and the same table says "**hold 8–12 weeks before judging recovery**." §5 pages 1–4 land day 31–60; pages 5–10 land day 61–90. Four weeks of post-rollout data cannot answer the question, and the honest answer at day 90 is "too early." **Do:** replace the day-90 criterion with leading indicators that *can* move by then (indexed-URL counts, sitemapped-URL coverage, `123g.us`/`h-source` occurrences at zero, `/tags/` occurrences at zero, homepage-only citation share, control-probe status) and move the citation-rate readout to day 150–180.

**B2. The canary has no control group, no success threshold and no rollback trigger.**
"Ship P0-5 + P0-6 together to ~200 card URLs across ≥3 categories. Hold 4 weeks." Shipping two large changes together means a negative result is uninterpretable. There is no matched holdout, no pre-registered metric, no threshold that would stop the rollout, and no rollback plan for a schema change the document itself calls "the one release most likely to move rankings in either direction." **Do:** matched-pair selection (200 treated, 200 held, matched on category, current impressions and depth), pre-register the metric and the stop threshold, and separate P0-5 from P0-6 by two weeks if the volume allows — or state explicitly that they are deliberately confounded and you accept a joint readout.

**B3. §7.4 pattern (1) is contradicted by §7.3 within the same document.**
"Sitemap inclusion **determines** whether a citable deep page exists… the strongest single argument for P1-3" (a "large" effort item). But #19 and #18 are won by `help.` pages, which the same row notes are "outside the sitemap entirely," and #20 is won by a `/tags/` URL that P1-3 proposes to *remove* from sitemaps. Four positive observations, at least three counterexamples, and an obvious confound (sitemapped hubs are presumably also the best internally linked). **Do:** demote to a hypothesis, state the counterexamples, and name the falsification test — #15 thank-you is proposed as a check but only in the confirming direction. Cheap discriminator: compare internal-link depth and inlink count for the four ranking hubs against four non-ranking ones from the gap-4 crawl. If depth explains it, P1-7/internal linking outranks P1-3.

**B4. The product is an email and email deliverability is never audited.**
§7.3 #18 records that users "report spam email and sharing addresses with advertisers," and P0-10(e) responds with a public sender-domain list and reclassification requests. Nowhere is there an audit of SPF, DKIM, DMARC (policy and reporting), BIMI, sending-IP and domain blocklist status, or compliance with the Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender requirements. If a meaningful share of cards land in spam, the brand's core "we deliver your card" promise is broken and no amount of citation work matters — and the safety narrative in #18 is downstream of it. DNS checks do not require fetching the blocked hosts. **Do:** add a deliverability layer to Week 1: DNS records for every sending domain, blocklist status, DMARC aggregate reports if any exist, and a request for ESP delivery/complaint/spam-rate stats. This is also the highest-leverage input to P0-10.

**B5. Audience geography and language are never established, and the whole PR plan assumes US.**
Tier 1–3 targets are US/UK publishers; 6.2.3 pitches AARP, seniorliving.org, A Place for Mom, DailyCaring; the benchmark is a single locale. The PR fact sheet in Tier 2 itself lists "language coverage" as a brand asset. For a free global ecard brand of this age, a large share of sessions may be non-US, and there is no hreflang, international-targeting or multilingual-URL assessment anywhere. If the traffic is majority non-US, the PR strategy, the seniors bet and the benchmark locale are all mis-targeted. **Do:** add geography/language to the gap-10 business call and to the GA4 ask, and add hreflang/i18n as an audit layer. Do not commission the seniors content until the geography is known.

**B6. Image and video discovery channels are absent from a catalogue of 30,000 images and a library of musical cards.**
No image sitemap, no alt-text assessment, no Google Images/Lens consideration, no image licensing/structured-data, no YouTube presence, no app-store listings or app deep-linking. §7.3 #25 identifies musical cards as the brand's strongest category and #3 notes format facets exist only on m. — yet the response is confined to VideoObject markup on card templates. For this product, image and video search are plausibly larger recoverable channels than any of the fifteen question pages. **Do:** add image/video sitemaps to P1-3's scope explicitly, add an image-SEO layer to the audit, and check for owned app and YouTube properties (they are also `sameAs` entity signals P1-1 needs).

**B7. Core Web Vitals / page experience is obtainable and absent.**
§8 gap 1 says every host is unreachable from the sandbox, and then correctly notes "Google's Rich Results Test also fetches live and bypasses this block." The same is true of the PageSpeed Insights API and the CrUX API/dataset — CrUX gives 28-day field LCP/INP/CLS per origin, for both www and m., historically, with no site access and no GSC. For an ad-heavy site with interstitials and anchor ads served to a 50–70 audience, INP and CLS are both a ranking input and the quantitative evidence P2-8 lacks. **Do:** pull CrUX for both origins plus the top three competitors in Week 1. It costs minutes and it partially closes gap 3's "cannot be evaluated."

**B8. "No competitive content exists" is treated as opportunity without asking why the market is empty.**
6.2.3 declares "No editorial roundup of ecard sites for older adults exists" from two probes on a capped index, and 6.2.4 declares a Reddit/Quora vacuum from three. Both are plausible. Neither considers the obvious explanation: ecards carry no affiliate revenue, so affiliate-driven senior verticals have no reason to publish roundups — which also means your pitch has no commercial hook for them, and the document's own Tier 3 note ("inclusion on merit alone is unlikely") makes exactly this argument about competitor blogs but not about publishers. The Reddit vacuum may simply mean people don't discuss ecards. **Do:** before commissioning, sanity-check demand (search volume for senior-ecard phrasings, subreddit post frequency on the "send something to my mother" pattern). If demand is ~zero, "whoever publishes first defines the answer" is true and worthless. Also state a pitch angle that pays the publisher — data, not a product mention.

**B9. Absence of paid and legal levers from a plan whose organic fixes read out in 8–12 weeks.**
The 123cards confusion (P0-10) is addressed solely with a disambiguation page. No brand-term PPC to defend "123greetings" against the misattributed billing complaints, no trademark/UDRP assessment, no Google Business/knowledge-panel claim path. For an audit whose own timeline says nothing measurable moves for a quarter, the absence of any same-week lever is a structural omission. **Do:** add a one-row paid recommendation (brand-term defence during the reputation remediation window) and a line on whether the 123cards similarity is actionable. Flag it as out-of-scope if it is, but say so deliberately.

**B10. The UGC layer is unaudited.**
P0-5 casually includes "top comments" in the server-render scope, which implies a large comment corpus. There is no assessment of comment volume, spam density, indexability, whether comment or user-submitted-card pages inflate the index, or whether UGC spam is a site-quality drag. P2-3 handles profile pages as a schema item only. For a 29-year-old site with an open UGC surface this is likely one of the largest URL populations and one of the larger quality risks. **Do:** add UGC to the gap-4 crawl scope and to the audit layers; decide indexation policy before P0-5 hard-codes comments into every card page.

**B11. Seasonal architecture is missing from a business that is seasonal.**
"Christmas is peak seasonality for the entire category" and "the ~12 'current events' sitemap URLs suggest seasonal hubs exist" — and that is the extent of it. No lifecycle policy: evergreen vs year-versioned URLs, what happens to a seasonal hub in February, whether last year's URLs 404 or redirect, when seasonal content must be live relative to the query peak (8–10 weeks out), or how the 90-day roadmap intersects the Q4 run-up. The roadmap ends at day 90 — which from a 23 September start lands at 22 December, i.e. the entire plan executes *through* peak season with no freeze window and no seasonal deliverable. That is a scheduling error, not just an omission. **Do:** add a seasonal-URL policy, pull the Christmas-adjacent work forward, and declare a change-freeze window around peak for the riskiest items (P0-6 rollout, P0-3 facet 404s, P1-3 sitemap replacement).

**B12. The PR workstream has no owner, no expected yield, no measurement and no propagation-lag caveat.**
Three tiers, roughly thirty named targets, effort "medium." No response-rate assumption, no tracker, no re-check cadence to confirm a correction actually landed, no statement of what happens when a publisher declines, and no acknowledgement that correcting a fact in a live page changes retrieval-grounded answers within weeks but does not touch parametric memory (which is exactly where the #4 result and the "since 1998" error live). The document is otherwise careful about this distinction and drops it precisely where it matters. **Do:** add expected conversion (a realistic figure is 10–25% on Tier 2 with a specific factual correction, near-zero on Tier 3 competitor-owned blogs), a tracker with a 6-week re-check, and a line stating which fixes propagate fast and which never will.

**B13. No capacity, cost or sequencing-against-reality check.**
Roughly forty-five items, owners named as functions ("Infra", "Content ops", "Revenue Ops", "WordPress"), three of them "large," with no FTE estimate, no cost, and — per gap 10 — no knowledge of whether the company has those functions or what its headcount band is. P0-5 alone (server-rendering the card player plus related links across the primary template) is a quarter of engineering work at many companies. **Do:** add capacity to the 30-minute business call, then publish a ranked "if you can only do five things" list. The closing note does three; extend it and make it the front of §9.

**B14. No risk register and no rollback plan.**
Risks live scattered in prose. Absent entirely: traffic loss from 404'ing 6,000–12,000 facet URLs and the `/do/card/` root (millions of already-sent emails and social shares may point at `/do/card/{id}` — the 301 is right, the root 404 is asserted with no inbound-volume check, and with no logs or GA4 there is currently no way to make that call); revenue loss from P2-8; rollback for P0-6; the possibility that de-duplicating hub listings and capping at ~60 (P1-7) removes indexed URLs; brand risk from the Reddit programme. **Do:** one table — risk, likelihood, blast radius, early-warning signal, rollback. Gate the `/do/card/` root 404 on the log export from P0-0.

**B15. Compliance layers never touched: accessibility, privacy, COPPA.**
Accessibility appears once, as a *content topic* for the seniors guide. It is never audited, despite a 50–70 core audience, interstitial ads, and a demand-letter climate for consumer sites. Ad-tech consent (GDPR/CCPA) is never mentioned although §7.3 #18 records users complaining about address-sharing with advertisers — which is a privacy-compliance question dressed as a reputation problem, and it is the same finding the audit uses to justify P0-10. Kids' ecards raise COPPA. **Do:** flag all three as unassessed layers with a recommendation to scope separately; at minimum connect the address-sharing complaint to the privacy policy review already implied by #18.

## What is genuinely solid — noted and not padded

- **§7.1 and §8.** The distinction between a grounded proxy and real engine output, the explicit "three prompts were not measured — do not read those rows as findings," and the thirteen-row gap table with "what would change" per row are better than most audits manage. §8's closing note on `votes_refuted: 0` — that findings "survived by not being challenged, not by withstanding challenge" — is unusually honest and should stay in verbatim.
- **The §7.2 decomposition.** Splitting 16 named prompts into real-page / homepage-only / no-URL is the finding, and the document is right to say so. It needs the non-brand cut (A7) but the structure is correct.
- **The #21 vs #22 contrast.** "Wins one conquest query on borrowed roundups and loses the other because no roundup happened to cover it" is the strongest piece of reasoning in the visible text and correctly motivates P1-9.
- **6.2.4's anti-astroturfing stance**, including the disclosure requirement and the specific subreddit list, is correct and correctly reasoned.
- **The closing three-item note** (P0-8 SQL update, BBB reply, `noarchive` grep) is the most useful paragraph in the document. It should be at the top, not the bottom.
- **6.2.5** correctly identifies that the subdomain question bounds every other recommendation and correctly refuses to order a migration before measurement exists. Keep the gate as written.
